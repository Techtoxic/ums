Part 1: Introduction

1.1 About This Manual

Welcome to the official user manual for the EDTTI Institute Management System (IMS). This guide is written for everyone who uses the system: students, trainers (lecturers), Heads of Department, the Dean, the Deputy Principal, Finance officers, the Registrar, the Industrial Liaison Office, the CBET (assessment) office, and System Administrators.

The manual is organised so you can read only the parts that matter to you. Part 1 and Part 2 apply to everyone; they cover what the system is and how to log in. After that, each role has its own dedicated part (for example, students should read Part 3, trainers Part 4, and so on). The later parts (12-17) cover features and topics that cut across all roles, such as notifications, book resources, troubleshooting, security, and a glossary of terms.

Throughout the manual you will see three kinds of highlighted boxes:

Note: Useful background information that helps you understand a feature.
Tip: A shortcut or piece of advice that makes a task easier.
Warning: Something that could cause data loss or cannot be undone. Read these carefully.

UI elements you click or type into are written in bold (for example, the Save button or the Email Address field). Web addresses, file names, and system values are written in code style.

Note: This manual describes the system exactly as it is built today. Some features that are common in other institute systems (online examination results/grades, transcripts, and class timetables) are not part of this version. Where you might expect those, the manual explains what the system uses instead (for example, competency-based assessment document uploads, and fees calculated from the program cost). Nothing in this manual is a placeholder; every instruction reflects a feature that actually exists.

1.2 About the EDTTI Institute Management System

The EDTTI Institute Management System (IMS) is a secure, web-based platform that runs entirely in your internet browser; there is nothing to install. It brings the day-to-day administration of the Institute into one place, replacing scattered paper records, spreadsheets, and manual processes.

The system is organised into portals. A portal is a private workspace tailored to one role: it shows only the tools and information that role needs. When you log in, the system automatically takes you to the correct portal for your account.

The system handles the following core areas of Institute life:

  • Admissions: registering new students and issuing admission numbers and login credentials.
  • Student records: personal details, course, department, module, and intake.
  • Fees and payments: recording payments, generating receipts, and showing each student their balance.
  • Unit (course) registration: students enrol in the units for their current module.
  • Competency-Based Education and Training (CBET) evidence: students upload assessment and practical work, which the CBET office reviews.
  • Teaching materials ("Tools of Trade"): trainers submit course outlines, session plans, records of work, and similar documents for review.
  • Book resources: trainers curate free online textbooks for each unit, and students read or download them.
  • Applications: students apply for graduation and industrial attachment, which the Industrial Liaison Office reviews.
  • Payroll: Finance generates payslips for trainers.
  • Notifications: the system keeps everyone informed of important events.

1.3 System Requirements

The system is designed to work on any modern device with an internet connection. You do not need to install any software.

  • Supported browsers: Use a current version of Google Chrome, Microsoft Edge, Mozilla Firefox, or Safari. For the best experience, keep your browser updated to the latest version. Internet Explorer is not supported.
  • Internet connection: A stable internet connection is required at all times while using the system. A basic broadband or mobile data connection is sufficient. Uploading documents (such as assessment PDFs) will be faster on a stronger connection.
  • Device compatibility: The system works on desktop and laptop computers, tablets, and smartphones. The layout adapts to the size of your screen. For tasks that involve a lot of typing or reviewing large tables (such as recording payments or reviewing applications), a laptop or desktop is more comfortable.
  • Other software: None. Everything runs in the browser. To open downloaded receipts and reports you will need a PDF viewer, which is built into all modern browsers and devices.
Tip: Allow your browser to keep you signed in for the duration of your session, but always log out on shared or public computers (see Section 2.8).

Part 2: Getting Started (All Users)

2.1 Accessing the System

The system is reached through a web address (URL) provided by your Institute, for example edtti.ac.ke. Open your browser, type the address into the address bar at the top, and press Enter.

You will arrive at the landing page (the public home page). This page introduces the Institute and provides the doorways into each portal. On the landing page you will find login buttons in the top navigation bar and in the page footer:

  • Student: for students
  • Trainer: for trainers (lecturers)
  • HOD: for Heads of Department
  • Staff Portal: for all other staff (Admin, Registrar, Finance, Dean, Deputy, ILO, CBET)
Tip: The landing page has a dark mode toggle (a moon/sun icon). Click it to switch between light and dark appearance; the system remembers your choice on that device.

2.2 Understanding the Login Process

The system uses two different login methods, depending on who you are. This is a deliberate security design.

  1. Single-step login: used by students, trainers, and Heads of Department. You enter your identifier and password, and you are signed in immediately.
  2. Two-step login with a One-Time Passcode (OTP): used by all other staff (Admin, Registrar, Finance, Dean, Deputy, ILO, CBET). After entering your email and password, the system emails you a six-digit code that you must enter to complete the login.
Note: A One-Time Passcode (OTP) is a short numeric code that is valid only once and only for a few minutes. The two-step method protects sensitive administrative accounts: even if someone learns a staff member's password, they cannot log in without also having access to that staff member's email inbox.

The system also lets you keep more than one portal open at the same time in the same browser (for example, a Finance tab and an Admin tab), and they will not interfere with each other.

2.3 Student & Trainer Login

Note: This section is for students and trainers.

For students:

  1. From the landing page, click Student (or go to the address /student/login).
  2. In the Admission Number field, type your admission number exactly as it appears on your admission letter (for example, BM5/2503/J26).
  3. In the Password field, type your password. For a brand-new student, your initial password is your phone number (the one recorded during admission). The system accepts common Kenyan formats, so 0712..., 712..., or 254712... will all work.
  4. Click Sign In.

On success, you are taken to your Student Portal dashboard. On your first login, the system will immediately ask you to set a new password before you can continue (see Section 2.5).

For trainers:

  1. From the landing page, click Trainer (or go to /trainer/login).
  2. In the Email Address field, type your work email.
  3. In the Password field, type your password. A newly created trainer account uses the temporary password trainer123.
  4. Click Sign In.

On your first login you will be required to set a new password (see Section 2.5).

If login fails, the system shows a message such as "Invalid email or password" (or "Invalid admission number or password" for students). Check for typos, make sure Caps Lock is off, and confirm you are on the correct login page for your role.

Note: Heads of Department log in slightly differently: they select their Department and enter a password rather than an email. Use the HOD button on the landing page (/hod/login).

2.4 Staff Login (Two-Step with OTP)

Note: This section is for Admin, Registrar, Finance, Dean, Deputy, ILO, and CBET staff.

All of these staff roles use the same Staff Portal login page and the same two-step process.

  1. From the landing page, click Staff Portal (or go to /admin/login).
  2. In the Email Address field, type your work email.
  3. In the Password field, type your password.
  4. Click Sign In. The system checks your credentials and, if they are correct, emails a six-digit verification code to your inbox.
  5. Switch to your email. Find the message titled "EDTTI Login Verification Code". The code is shown in the body of the email.
  6. Return to the login page. The screen now shows six boxes for the code. Type the six digits.
  7. Click to verify. You are signed in and taken to your portal.

Important details about the OTP:

  • The code is valid for 10 minutes. If it expires, start the login again to receive a fresh code.
  • You have a maximum of 5 attempts to enter a code correctly. After that, the code is cancelled and you must request a new one by logging in again.
  • The code is single-use; once it works, it cannot be used again.

If the OTP email does not arrive:

  1. Wait up to a minute or two; email can be slightly delayed.
  2. Check your Spam/Junk folder.
  3. Confirm you typed your email correctly.
  4. If the system could not send the email at all, it will tell you with a message like "We could not send your verification code right now. Please try again in a moment." In that case, wait and try logging in again.
  5. If the problem persists, contact the System Administrator (see Appendix B).
Warning: Never forward your OTP email or read your code aloud to anyone. The code grants access to your account.

2.5 First-Time Login

Note: This section applies to new students and new trainers.

When your account is brand-new, the system forces you to replace your initial password with one only you know. This happens automatically the first time you log in and before you can use any other feature.

  1. Log in with your initial credentials as described in Section 2.3.
  2. A window titled "Set a new password" appears over the login page.
  3. In the New password field, type a strong new password (see Section 2.6 for the rules).
  4. In the Confirm new password field, type the same password again.
  5. Click Save & Continue.

Once your new password is accepted, you are taken straight into your portal. From then on you log in with your new password.

Note: Staff accounts (Admin, Registrar, Finance, Dean, Deputy, ILO, CBET) do not use this first-login screen. Their accounts are set up ready to use, and they sign in with the two-step OTP process described in Section 2.4.
Tip: Choose a password you can remember but others cannot guess. Do not reuse your phone number or admission number.

2.6 Password Requirements

To keep accounts secure, every new password (whether set at first login or changed later) must meet these rules:

  • At least 8 characters long.
  • At least one lowercase letter (a-z).
  • At least one uppercase letter (A-Z).
  • At least one number (0-9).
  • At least one special character (for example !, @, #, $, %, *, ?, &).
  • It must be different from your current/old password.

An example of a password that meets all the rules is Edtti2026! (do not use this exact example; choose your own).

Warning: If your new password does not meet every rule, the system will reject it and explain what is missing. Adjust it and try again.

2.7 Forgotten Password / Password Reset

If you forget your password, you can reset it yourself using the Forgot Password flow. This is available from the login pages.

  1. On your login page, click the Forgot Password? link.
  2. Enter the email address associated with your account.
  3. Choose how you want to receive your reset: a one-time code (OTP) or a reset link.
  4. Click to send. The system emails you either a six-digit code ("EDTTI Password Reset - One-Time Password") or a reset link ("EDTTI Password Reset - Reset Link").
  5. If you chose the code: enter the six-digit code on the next screen, then set your new password (following the rules in Section 2.6).
  6. If you chose the link: open the link in the email and set your new password on the page it opens.

Time limits:

  • The reset code (OTP) is valid for 10 minutes.
  • The reset link is valid for 60 minutes and can be used only once.
Note: For security, the system always responds with the same "If an account exists, instructions have been sent" message; it will not reveal whether a particular email is registered. To prevent abuse, you can request at most 3 password resets every 15 minutes.

Who can reset a password:

  • Every user (student, trainer, and all staff) resets their own password using the Forgot Password flow above. This requires access to the email address on the account.
  • Students whose accounts have no email on file should contact the Registrar for help. Staff should contact the System Administrator.
Note: The system does not provide a "reset someone else's password" button. Password resets are self-service through email. If a student cannot receive the reset email, the correct path is to have the Registrar confirm and update the student's email address on record.

2.8 Logging Out

When you finish working, log out to protect your account.

  1. Find the Logout button. In every portal it is in the sidebar (often at the bottom) or the top-right corner.
  2. Click Logout. You may be asked to confirm.
  3. You are returned to the login page, and your session ends on that device.
Warning: Always log out when using a shared or public computer (such as a library or cyber café). If you simply close the browser without logging out, the next person may be able to reopen your session.
Note: Logging out of one portal does not log you out of any other portal you have open in the same browser. Each portal has its own independent session.

2.9 Navigating the System

Each role has its own portal, and you are taken to yours automatically after login. Within a portal, the main areas are arranged as tabs (sometimes shown as a sidebar menu). Clicking a tab switches the main area to that feature without reloading the whole page, so navigation is fast.

Common elements you will see in every portal:

  • A sidebar or top navigation listing the tabs available to your role.
  • A page title showing which tab you are on.
  • An academic year chip (for example, "AY 2025/2026") indicating the current academic year.
  • For most portals, a live clock and a dark-mode toggle.

Session expiry: For security, your session lasts about 2 hours. If you stay signed in but inactive for a long time, or if your session expires, the system will return you to the login page the next time you try to do something. Simply log in again to continue.

Tip: You can bookmark your portal's address (for example, /finance/dashboard). When you open the bookmark, if your session has expired you will be asked to log in first, then taken to that page.
Warning: If you are filling in a form (such as recording a payment or admitting a student) and your session expires before you click Save/Submit, your unsaved entries may be lost. Save your work regularly.

Part 3: Student Portal

Note: Every section in Part 3 is for students only.

3.1 Accessing the Student Portal

Open the student login page (/student/login), sign in with your admission number and password (see Section 2.3), and complete the first-login password change if prompted (see Section 2.5). After signing in, you arrive at your Dashboard.

Your Student Portal has ten areas, shown in the sidebar in this order: Dashboard, Profile, Financial Info, Payment History, Uploads, Notes & Messages, Units & Courses, My Books, Apply for Graduation, Apply for Attachment. The footer of the sidebar has a Dark Mode toggle and a Logout button.

3.2 Dashboard

The Dashboard is your home screen and gives you a quick summary of your fees and recent activity. It is for viewing only; there are no buttons to click here.

You will see:

  • A welcome card with your name and your current module number.
  • Three summary cards: Total Program Cost (the fee for your current module), Total Paid (the total of all payments recorded for you, in Kenya Shillings), and Outstanding Balance (what remains to be paid).
  • A Payment Progress bar showing how much of your current module's fee you have paid, as a percentage.
  • A Recent Activity list showing your last few payments.
Note: The fee shown is for one module at a time, not the full year. EDTTI bills fees per module, with three modules per year. See Section 3.4 for how the balance is calculated.

3.3 My Profile

The Profile tab shows your personal and academic information.

You will see:

  • Your name, admission number, and avatar.
  • Personal details: ID Number, Phone Number, Email Address, KCSE Grade.
  • Academic details: Course, Department, Module, Intake, and Admission Type (shown as "Walk-in" or "KUCCPS").

What you can change: You can update only your Email Address.

  1. Next to Email Address, click Update.
  2. In the window that opens, type your New Email Address.
  3. Click Update Email. A confirmation message appears and your profile refreshes.
Note: All other fields (your name, ID number, phone number, KCSE grade, course, department, module, and intake) are read-only. If any of these is wrong, contact the Registrar to have it corrected (see Appendix B). The student cannot change these directly.

3.4 Fee Statement & Payments

Note: EDTTI does not use a separate "fee structure" screen. Your fee is derived from your program's cost. The program cost stored in the system is the annual fee; because there are three modules per year, the fee for one module is the annual cost divided by three (rounded up). You are billed one module at a time.

The Financial Info tab gives you the detailed breakdown:

  • Three cards: Program Cost (your per-module fee), Total Paid, and Balance.
  • A Payment Progress bar with a percentage.
  • A Program Details panel: Program Name, Department, Module, Program Cost, Amount Paid, and Outstanding.

How your balance is calculated:

  • Program Cost (per module) = annual program cost ÷ 3 (rounded up).
  • Total Paid = the sum of every payment recorded against your account.
  • Balance = Program Cost (per module) minus Total Paid. If you have overpaid, the balance can show as a negative (credit) amount.

Viewing your payment history and receipts is done on the Payment History tab:

  1. Open Payment History. Each payment is listed with its amount, date, and a small tag showing the method (M-Pesa, Bank, or Payment).
  2. Click any payment to open its receipt.
  3. The receipt window shows your name, admission number, course, department, academic year, amount paid, payment method, reference number, and date.
  4. Use Export Receipt to save the receipt as a PDF, or Print to print it on the official letterhead.

Understanding payment statuses: Every payment shown has already been recorded and confirmed by the Finance office; there are no "pending" payments visible to you. A payment appears here only after a Finance officer enters it.

If a payment is missing: Payments are entered by the Finance office, not by students. If a payment you made does not appear within a reasonable time, contact Finance and provide your payment reference number (the M-Pesa transaction ID, bank slip number, or bursary reference). See Section 15.5.

3.5 Course / Unit Registration

The Units & Courses tab is where you see your course units and register for the ones in your current module.

You will see:

  • A header with counts: Total Units, Department units, Common units, Registered, Unregistered, and History.
  • A banner showing whether registration is available or blocked, based on your outstanding balance.
  • Unit cards for your current module, each tagged as a regular or Common unit, with a status of Registered, Not Registered, or Blocked.
  • A collapsible History: Past Module Units section listing units from earlier modules (these are shown for reference and cannot be registered again).

When registration is open: Registration depends on your fee balance. The Finance office sets a fee threshold (a minimum payment level). If your outstanding balance is below that threshold, registration is available; if it is at or above the threshold, registration is blocked until you pay enough to come under it.

How to register for units:

  1. Open Units & Courses.
  2. Check the banner at the top. If it reads Registration Available, you may proceed. If it reads Registration Blocked, pay enough fees to come under the threshold first.
  3. To register a single unit, click Register on that unit's card.
  4. To register several units at once, click Register Unregistered Units in the banner, tick the units you want in the list that appears, and click Register Selected Units.

Dropping a unit: The system does not provide a self-service "drop unit" button. If you registered for a unit in error, contact the Registrar.

Viewing registered units: Registered units show a Registered status pill on their card, and the count of registered units appears in the header.

Note: Common units are shared across several programs (for example, communication or numeracy skills). They appear alongside your department units. You cannot upload assessment evidence for common units (see Section 3.10).

3.6 Examination Results

Note: This version of the EDTTI system does not include an online examination results or grades module. There is no place in the Student Portal to view marks, grades, GPA, or pass/fail outcomes, because the system does not store them.

EDTTI follows a Competency-Based Education and Training (CBET) model. Instead of viewing graded exam results online, you demonstrate competency by uploading assessment and practical evidence for each unit, which the CBET office reviews (see Section 3.10). Formal results and competency outcomes are communicated to you through the Institute's normal academic channels (your trainer, department, or the Registrar), not through this portal.

If you have a question about your results or competency status, contact your trainer or the Registrar (see Section 15.6).

3.7 Transcript

Note: This version of the system does not generate transcripts. There is no transcript tab in the Student Portal.

To request an official academic record or transcript, contact the Registrar's office directly (see Appendix B). The Registrar maintains the authoritative academic records for all students.

3.8 Timetable / Class Schedule

Note: This version of the system does not include an online class timetable. There is no timetable tab in the Student Portal, and the system does not store scheduling data.

Class schedules are distributed through your department and trainers. Contact your Head of Department or trainer for your current class schedule.

3.9 Notes & Messages

The Notes & Messages tab is where you read official notices addressed to students from the Dean's office.

You will see:

  • Filter tabs: All, Unread (with a count badge), and Read.
  • Each note shown as a card with a category icon, a title, a priority tag (for example, urgent, high, normal, low), a short preview, and the date. New (unread) notes are marked.

To read and manage notes:

  1. Open Notes & Messages.
  2. Click a note card to open it in full. Opening a note automatically marks it as read.
  3. Use the All / Unread / Read filters to focus on what you need.
  4. Click Refresh to check for new notes. The system also checks automatically every few minutes.
Note: Notes shown here are public notices from the Dean (for example, welfare announcements). They are read-only; students cannot reply or post notes. Private staff notes about a student are not visible to the student. The system also sends you in-app notifications about other events (such as your graduation or attachment application status); see Part 12.

3.10 Uploaded Documents

The Uploads tab is where you submit required documents and your competency-based assessment and practical evidence.

At the top you will find three personal document slots:

  • Profile Photo: accepted as JPG or PNG, up to about 2 MB.
  • KCSE Results: accepted as PDF or image, up to about 5 MB.
  • KCPE Results: accepted as PDF or image, up to about 5 MB.

Below these, for each unit you are registered in, you will find assessment slots:

  • Assessment 1, 2, and 3: for all units.
  • Practical 1, 2, and 3 and a Combined Video: for department (non-common) units only.

How to upload a document:

  1. Open Uploads.
  2. Find the slot you need (for example, Assessment 1 under a particular unit).
  3. Click Upload and choose the file from your device.
  4. Wait for the confirmation. The slot's status changes to Uploaded and shows the file name, date, and version.

File rules to remember:

  • Assessment files are accepted as PDF or Word (DOCX).
  • Practical files must be PDF.
  • The system checks the actual content of each file (not just its name), so renaming a file will not bypass the type check.
  • The overall maximum file size is 10 MB (images are limited to 5 MB).
  • You can only upload assessments for units you are registered in, and not for common units.

Replacing and viewing: You can replace a previously uploaded file (the old version is kept as a record and marked "replaced"; uploads are never permanently deleted) and view any file you have uploaded.

What happens next: Your uploaded assessment evidence becomes available to the CBET (CIBEC) office, which reviews students' assessment and practical work for completeness (see Section 11.6 for the CBET review process, handled by the assessment office).

    • Full Name *
    • ID/Birth Certificate Number *
    • Phone Number * (10 digits, for example 0712345678)
    • Email (optional, but recommended so the student receives their credentials by email)
    • Admission Type * (Walk-in or KUCCPS)

Programs (the academic offerings) are part of the catalog. The Registrar has program access through the shared catalog used across the system. Program cost is typically set in the Finance portal (Section 8.4). Creating or editing programs is an academic-structure task shared with Admin.

9.5 Managing Units / Courses

Units (the subjects within programs) are likewise part of the shared catalog. The Registrar can view units across courses and departments. Common units (units shared across several programs) are managed through the common-units feature used by HODs and the catalog.

9.6 Academic Period Management

The academic period is controlled through system settings rather than a dedicated semester screen:

  • The academic year is calculated automatically (September-August) and cannot be set manually.
  • The current intake (January, May, or September) is set in the Finance or Admin Settings tab.

Changing the current intake affects new admission numbering and the labelling of the active period.

9.7 Timetable Management

Note: This version of the system does not include timetable management. There is no timetable tab in the Registrar Portal.

9.8 Results & Transcript Management

Note: This version of the system does not store exam results or generate transcripts. The Registrar maintains authoritative academic records outside this system. Student competency evidence is collected through assessment uploads and reviewed by the CBET office.

9.9 Student Promotion

The Student Promotion tab advances students one module within their level's cap.

  1. Open Student Promotion.
  2. Use the Level, Module, and Department filters and click Load Students.
  3. Review the list; each row shows the student's current module, level, the maximum module for their level, and whether they are eligible.
  4. Tick the students to promote (or use the select-all checkbox), then click Promote Selected. You can also promote a single student from their row.
Note: A student cannot be promoted beyond their level's maximum module (Level 3 caps at Module 1, Level 4 at 2, Level 5 at 4, Level 6 at 6). The system blocks promotion past the cap.

9.10 Password Reset for Students

The system does not provide a button for the Registrar to directly reset a student's password. Password resets are self-service via the Forgot Password? link on the login page (see Section 2.7), which requires the student's email.

If a student cannot receive the reset email because no (or an incorrect) email is on file, the Registrar's role is to update the student's email address in Student Management (via Edit) so the student can then use Forgot Password.

9.11 Reports & Exports

The Registrar can export student data using the Export button (in the top bar and on the Student Admission tab). The export window lets you choose what to export (all students, by department/module/intake, by admission type, or custom filters) and the format (CSV, JSON, or PDF).

There is no separate notifications tab in the Registrar Portal; actions you take show on-screen confirmations. New admissions trigger a credentials email to the student (where an email is on file).

Part 13: Book Resources Module

13.1 Overview

The Book Resources module gives students free, curated reading material for their units. Trainers choose suitable books from large open libraries and approve them to specific units; students then read those books inside the portal or download them. This means students always have relevant, vetted reading without searching the open internet.

13.2 For Trainers: Approving Books

Note: This subsection is for trainers.

Trainers approve books on the Unit Books tab (see Section 4.11 for the full steps). In summary:

  1. Open Unit Books and pick the Unit.
  2. Search for a topic, title, or author (optionally use the deep filters).
  3. Preview a book in the built-in reader to confirm it is suitable.
  4. Click Add to Unit to approve it (or Remove to withdraw a previously approved book).

The moment you approve a book, students registered in that unit can see it.

13.3 For Students: Accessing Books

Note: This subsection is for students.

Students read books on the My Books tab (see Section 3.13):

  1. Open My Books.
  2. Find a book under one of your units.
  3. Click Read to open it in the in-portal reader, or use the download icon to save the PDF (when available).

13.4 Supported Sources

The module draws on two free, reputable open libraries:

  • OpenStax: peer-reviewed, openly licensed academic textbooks (for example, physics, biology, business). These typically include full downloadable PDFs.
  • Open Library: a very large catalogue of books, including many technical and vocational titles, with readable copies hosted on the Internet Archive.

When searching, trainers can filter the source to Academic Textbooks (OpenStax) or Library Books (Open Library).

13.5 Limitations

A few things are worth knowing about how books behave:

  • The module depends on these external libraries. If one is temporarily unavailable, search may return fewer results and show a notice that some sources could not be reached; the other source still works.
  • Not every book can be downloaded. Some titles can be read in the in-portal reader but do not offer a downloadable PDF (in that case the download icon is disabled). OpenStax textbooks generally do offer full PDFs.
  • Students only ever see books for the units they are registered in and that a trainer has approved for that unit.
Tip: If you (a trainer) cannot find a suitable book, try broader search terms or switch the Source filter. If you (a student) see no books, it means none have been approved yet for your registered units.

Part 14: Common Tasks & Workflows

These workflows show how the system works end-to-end, across more than one portal.

14.1 Workflow: New Student Admission to First Login

This is the journey from admitting a student to that student using the portal.

  1. Registrar opens the Registrar Portal and clicks Add Student (see Section 9.2).
  2. The Registrar fills in the student's details and submits.
  3. The system generates the admission number automatically (for example, BM5/2503/J26) and sets the student's initial password to their phone number.
  4. The system emails the student their admission number and initial password (if an email is on file); otherwise the Registrar gives the student the details, and can print the admission letter.
  5. The student opens the student login page and signs in with the admission number and phone number.
  6. The system forces a password change; the student sets a new, strong password (see Section 2.5).
  7. The student lands on their Dashboard and can now register for units (see Section 3.5), upload documents, and apply for graduation/attachment when eligible.

14.2 Workflow: Fee Payment Recording & Student Confirmation

This is how a fee payment moves from the cashier's desk to the student's screen.

  1. The student pays fees (for example, by M-Pesa or bank transfer) and keeps the reference number (M-Pesa transaction ID or bank slip number).
  2. The Finance officer opens the Finance Portal Dashboard, finds the student, and clicks Add Payment (see Section 8.2).
  3. The officer selects the payment mode, enters the reference and amount, and submits.
  4. The system records the payment (rejecting it if the same reference was already entered) and updates the student's balance instantly.
  5. The student opens their Financial Info and Payment History tabs and sees the updated balance and a receipt they can download or print.

14.3 Workflow: Competency Assessment Evidence (CBET)

Note: This system does not publish exam results. The equivalent end-to-end process is the submission and review of competency evidence, shown here.
  1. The trainer delivers the unit and curates reading material via Unit Books (Section 4.11).
  2. The student completes assessments and practicals and uploads the evidence in the Uploads tab for each registered unit (Section 3.10).
  3. The system notifies the CBET (CIBEC) office of each upload.
  4. A CBET officer opens the CBET dashboard and reviews the evidence by unit, checking the completeness view to see which students have submitted all required slots and which are missing (Section 11.6).
  5. Competency outcomes are then handled through the Institute's academic processes.

14.4 Workflow: Industrial Attachment Application

This is how a student requests attachment and the ILO records the decision.

  1. The student, once eligible (at the final module of their level), opens Apply for Attachment, chooses a county and nearest town, and submits (Section 3.12).
  2. The system notifies the ILO of the new application.
  3. An ILO officer opens Attachment Applications, reviews the request, adds any comments, and sets the status to Approved, Under Review, or Rejected (Section 11.2).
  4. The student is notified and can see the status and comments on their Apply for Attachment tab.

14.5 Workflow: New Trainer Onboarding

This is how a new trainer goes from account creation to teaching.

  1. The Admin opens the Admin Portal Trainers tab and clicks Add Trainer, entering the trainer's name, email, and department (Section 10.4).
  2. The system creates the account with the temporary password trainer123 and flags it for a first-login password change.
  3. The trainer opens the trainer login page, signs in with their email and trainer123, and sets a new password (Section 2.5).
  4. The HOD assigns units to the trainer (Section 5.3a).
  5. The trainer opens My Assignments and My Students, can Export Attendance, curate Unit Books, and submit Tools of Trade.

Part 15: Troubleshooting

15.1 I Cannot Log In

Work through this checklist:

  1. Caps Lock: make sure it is off; passwords are case-sensitive.
  2. Correct login page: students use /student/login, trainers /trainer/login, HODs /hod/login, and all other staff the Staff Portal (/admin/login). Logging in on the wrong page will not work.
  3. Correct identifier: students use their admission number, trainers and staff use their email, HODs select their department.
  4. New account: if it is your first login, your initial password is your phone number (students) or trainer123 (trainers); you will be asked to change it.
  5. Account status: if your account has been deactivated, contact the Administrator.
  6. Staff OTP: if you are staff and reach the code step, see Section 15.2.

15.2 I Did Not Receive the OTP Email

  1. Wait one to two minutes; email can be delayed.
  2. Check your Spam/Junk folder.
  3. Confirm your email address is correct.
  4. If the login screen says the code could not be sent, wait a moment and try logging in again to trigger a fresh code.
  5. Remember the code is valid for 10 minutes and allows 5 attempts; if it expires or is used up, request a new one by logging in again.
  6. If it still does not arrive, contact the System Administrator.

15.3 My Session Expired

Sessions last about 2 hours. If yours expires, the system returns you to the login page when you next act. Simply log in again to continue.

Warning: If you were part-way through a form (admitting a student, recording a payment, etc.) and had not yet saved, those entries may be lost. Re-enter and save them.

15.4 I Can See a Feature is Missing or Broken

First confirm the feature actually exists in this version; this manual notes several features (exam results, transcripts, timetables) that are not part of the system. If a feature that should be there is missing or not working:

  1. Refresh the page.
  2. Log out and back in.
  3. If it persists, report it (see Section 1.4) with: the portal and tab, the steps you took, what you expected, what happened, and a screenshot.

15.5 A Payment I Made Is Not Showing

Payments are entered by the Finance office, not by students, and appear in your portal once recorded. If a payment is missing:

  1. Allow time for Finance to process it.
  2. Contact the Finance office and provide your payment reference number (M-Pesa transaction ID, bank slip number, or bursary reference) and the date and amount.

15.6 My Results Are Wrong or Missing

This system does not display exam results or grades online (see Section 3.6). For any question about your results or competency status, contact your trainer or the Registrar through the Institute's normal channels.

15.7 I Cannot Upload a File

  1. Check the file type is accepted (for example, assessments must be PDF or DOCX; practicals must be PDF; photos must be JPG/PNG).
  2. Check the file size: the limit is 10 MB overall (images 5 MB; teaching "Tools of Trade" 1 MB).
  3. Make sure you are uploading to a registered unit (you cannot upload assessments for unregistered or common units).
  4. Try a stronger internet connection.
  5. If it still fails, note the exact error and contact support.

15.8 A Page is Showing an Error

If a page shows a friendly error screen ("Something went wrong" or "Page not found") with a reference code:

  1. Use the Back to Home button on the error page, or refresh.
  2. If the error keeps happening, report it with the reference code shown and the steps that led to it (see Section 1.4).
Note: The system never shows raw technical errors to users; if you see a clean error page with a reference number, simply share that number with support.

Part 16: Security & Privacy

16.1 Keeping Your Account Secure

Your account protects your personal information, so treat it carefully:

  • Choose a strong password (see Section 2.6) and never reuse your phone number or admission number.
  • Never share your password or your one-time codes (OTPs) with anyone, not even staff.
  • Log out when you finish, especially on shared or public computers (see Section 2.8).
  • Be wary of anyone asking for your login details by phone or email; the Institute will never ask for your password.

16.2 What Data the System Holds About You

For students, the system holds: your name, admission number, ID/birth-certificate number, KCSE grade, phone number, email, course, department, module, intake, next-of-kin details, your fee payments and balance, your unit registrations, your uploaded documents (photo, KCSE/KCPE results, assessment and practical evidence), and your graduation/attachment applications.

For staff, the system holds: your name, staff ID, role, department, email, phone, and (for trainers) your unit assignments and payslips.

16.3 Who Can See Your Data

Access is strictly limited by role:

  • Students can see only their own records.
  • Trainers can see the students in their assigned units (names, admission numbers, contact details) for attendance, and their own payslips and assignments.
  • HODs see information for their department.
  • Finance sees fee and payment information and trainer payslips.
  • Registrar/Admin manage student and account records.
  • Dean sees student welfare information and notes.
  • ILO sees graduation and attachment applications; CBET officers see students' uploaded assessment evidence.

The system enforces these boundaries on every request, so one student cannot view another student's records, and roles cannot reach data outside their remit.

16.4 Reporting a Security Concern

If you believe your account has been accessed by someone else, or you have shared your password by mistake:

  1. Change your password immediately (use Forgot Password if you cannot log in).
  2. Log out of all sessions.
  3. Report it to the System Administrator straight away (see Appendix B), describing what happened and when.

Part 17: Glossary

  • Academic Period / Academic Year: The yearly cycle the Institute operates on. In this system the academic year is calculated automatically and runs September-August.
  • Admission Number: Your unique student identifier, for example BM5/2503/J26 (course code / sequence number / intake code). Used as your username.
  • Attachment (Industrial Attachment): A period of supervised work placement in industry. Students apply through the portal; the ILO reviews applications.
  • CAT (Continuous Assessment Test): A common term for in-course assessment. Note: this system records competency evidence through uploads, not CAT marks.
  • CBET (Competency-Based Education and Training): An approach where students demonstrate competency through assessments and practicals rather than only written exams.
  • CIBEC / CBET Officer: The role and portal responsible for reviewing students' uploaded competency evidence.
  • Common Unit: A unit shared across several programs (for example, communication or numeracy skills).
  • Dean: The Dean of Students, responsible for student welfare in this system.
  • Deputy: The Deputy Principal, who has institution-wide oversight and manages Tools of Trade.
  • Fee Threshold: The maximum outstanding balance a student may have and still register for units; set by Finance/Admin.
  • HOD (Head of Department): The staff member who assigns units to trainers and oversees a department.
  • ILO (Industrial Liaison Office): The office that handles graduation and attachment applications.
  • IMS (Institute Management System): This system; the web platform described in this manual.
  • Intake: The entry cohort: January, May, or September, encoded in the admission number (J/M/S).
  • KCSE / KCPE: Kenyan national examinations; the grade is recorded at admission and uploaded as a document.
  • Module: A stage of a program. Students progress through modules; each level has a maximum module.
  • OTP (One-Time Passcode): A six-digit code, valid for 10 minutes and one use, emailed to staff to complete login or to anyone resetting a password.
  • Portal: A role-specific workspace within the system (Student Portal, Finance Portal, etc.).
  • Program / Course: An academic offering (such as Business Management Level 5). The program cost drives student fees.
  • Program Cost: The annual fee for a program; the per-module fee is this amount divided by three.
  • Session: Your active signed-in period, which lasts about two hours.
  • Tools of Trade: Teaching documents (course outline, learning plan, record of work, session plan, exam, TVET license) that trainers submit and the Deputy reviews.
  • Transcript: An official academic record; not generated by this system (request from the Registrar).
  • Unit / Course / Subject: A single subject within a program that students register for and trainers teach.
  • Walk-in / KUCCPS: The two admission types: a direct (walk-in) admission, or one placed through the Kenya Universities and Colleges Central Placement Service.
